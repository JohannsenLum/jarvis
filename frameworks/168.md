# The 168-Hour Budget
> Every week is exactly 168 hours. Budget it like money — zero-based, non-negotiables funded first, work gets the residual.

## What it is
The core time framework of this system: treat the week as a fixed capital budget of 168 hours and allocate it top-down, the way a CFO allocates cash. Most people budget backwards — work claims the week first and sleep, spouse, family, faith, and friends fight over the scraps. This framework inverts that: fund what cannot be outsourced and the relationships that matter FIRST, and only then hand the remainder to work. The remainder is still enormous — typically ~60 hours, more than a standard 40-hour job — so ambition never requires abandoning your life; it requires allocation.

## The framework

### 1. Zero-based subtraction, in strict order
Start from 168 every week and subtract in this order (hours below are the teaching example, not a mandate — set your own numbers):

| Order | Category | Example hours | Running total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sleep — the first thing that cannot be outsourced | ~40 | 128 |
| 2 | Health & grooming (gym, haircut, therapy) | ~8 | 120 |
| 3 | Faith / God (church, inner life) | ~6 | 114 |
| 4 | Spouse — a fixed date night plus a weekly "partnership progression" planning session | ~8 | 106 |
| 5 | Family — fixed weekend dinners | ~8 | 98 |
| 6 | Social — roughly one friend a week | ~6 | 92 |
| 7 | Commute (design it down: live near where you work, buy time) | ~10 | 82 |
| 8 | Admin | ~10 | 72 |
| 9 | Personal development | ~10 | 62 |
| 10 | **Work gets the residual** | **~60** | ~0 (the example hours are rounded; the exact residual here is 62, which the source rounds to "sixty") |

Rows 1–2 are the non-outsourceables; rows 3–6 are the relationship non-negotiables, allocated BEFORE work ever appears. Meals are not a category: eat with someone relevant and write the hour off into work or relationship budget.

### 2. The work-line taxonomy — "work" is never one bucket
The ~60 residual work hours are split into named budget lines, re-weighted each business season:
1. **Deals** — the core revenue function ("how many hours do I spend bringing a deal in?")
2. **Business development** — targeted pursuit of specific opportunities
3. **Partnership development** — building alliances; explicitly not sales ("它不是 sales 来的" [it's not sales])
4. **System building** — operations, building the machine
5. **People management / development** — partners and key people
6. **Ideation & research**
7. **Networking** — distinct from BD: BD is targeted, networking is going out to "哈喽哈喽" [hello-hello] and get introduced

### 3. Color-tagged calendar and multi-counting
Every calendar entry carries a color per business/domain, so the week is auditable at a glance (or by an AI). Run a weekly cadence: each major commitment gets standing recurring blocks (e.g., one for partners, one for key people), and lunch/dinner blocks are deliberately opened up for people. One meeting is engineered to multi-count across several budget lines at once — "It covers social, it covers business, it covers the building, everything."

### 4. The cascade: year → quarter → week, and hours don't roll over
- **Year:** write every known key date (birthdays, holidays, pre-planned commitments) onto 12 calendar pages up front — so nothing "ambushes" you that you actually knew about all year.
- **Quarter:** list the immovable commitments for the next 90 days and count the blocks genuinely left.
- **Week:** budget hours across a four-week breakdown, tabled against your quarterly goals.
- **Hard rule:** "这个礼拜没有，下个礼拜不回来了" [this week gone doesn't come back next week] — time is a perishing budget, never a rollover balance.

### 5. Season of war — extremity as a declared contract
Allocations are seasonal. In a fight season you may cut protected categories (e.g., family + friends ≈ 14 hours/week reclaimed), but only under five conditions:
1. **Name the season** ("this is war, season of war")
2. **Name the cut** (which categories, how many hours)
3. **Name the duration** (e.g., three months — a season, never a lifestyle)
4. **Announce it to the affected people** ("Hey, there will be no gathering or meeting for the next three months. I'm going to fight.")
5. **Schedule the repayment** ("以后补给他们" [repay them afterwards])

This converts what most people do guiltily and silently (ghosting family under pressure) into a transparent, bounded, reversible contract.

### 6. Master the box before you earn the flow
The execution layer is **time boxing** (fixed daily boxes) — stricter than loose time blocking — held for years (the teacher held it 8–10 years) before deliberately dropping it for priority-driven flow, and only because his season changed (international deal timing across time zones: "I cannot time box. I must flow."). Flow is a graduate privilege earned after structure is fully internalized; quoting "I must flow" without the years of boxing is how people justify chaos.

## When to apply
- Weekly and quarterly planning; onboarding into this system for the first time.
- Whenever the user says they have "no time" for family, health, faith, or a key relationship — that is an allocation failure, not a time shortage.
- When a crunch is starting (launch, sprint, fundraise): trigger the season-of-war protocol instead of silent overwork.
- Each new quarter or business season: re-weight the work-line taxonomy against current goals.
- When the user feels everything is urgent and days blur: the perpetual-urgency feeling is a symptom of unallocated time.

## How your AI uses this
1. **First run:** don't optimize immediately — have the user map reality for about three weeks first ("take three weeks to understand, map it out"), then table their 168 against their quarterly goals.
2. **Enforce the order:** when building or reviewing a weekly budget, always fund sleep, health, faith, spouse, family, and key relationships before any work allocation. If work appears first in a plan, flag it and re-sequence.
3. **Audit for drift:** review the calendar against the declared 168 budget weekly; report which categories are being silently robbed and where actuals diverge from the budget. Log unspent allocated hours as lost — never as rolled over.
4. **Break "work" open:** never accept "work: 60h" as a line item. Ask the user to split it across the seven work lines and re-weight per season; suggest multi-count opportunities (which buckets can one meeting serve simultaneously?).
5. **Season-of-war checks:** if the user is entering a crunch, walk the five conditions — season named? cut named? duration set? affected people told? repayment scheduled? Refuse to treat an undeclared, open-ended crunch as a "season." Track "current season" as a state variable that re-weights the allocation template.
6. **Stage-match the discipline:** recommend time boxing and color-tagged calendars for users still building the habit; do not endorse "flow" mode unless the user has demonstrably mastered boxing over years.
