# Declarations
> Write the future as present-tense states of being — "I am…", never "I will…" — read them daily, and move yourself toward the anchors.

## What it is
The goal-setting framework of this system: goals are never written as tasks or targets. They are written as **declarations** — present-tense statements of a state of being ("I am free from addiction," not "I will quit smoking") — collected into one document that tells a story about your future rather than listing wishes. The document spans every life pillar (health, wealth, marriage, family, friendships, business authority, ministry, philanthropy, legacy) and is read on a fixed ritual. The causal theory is explicit and non-mystical: a written declaration becomes your true north star, and *you move yourself towards it*. In this system the declaration set is the "north star" layer of personal context, sitting between your history (roots) and your current season.

## The framework

### 1. Declaration form — state of being, never task
Write the destination identity, in the present tense: "I am extremely healthy… I'm a very good swimmer," "I am free from addiction," "I have a beautiful and amazing smile." Never "I will quit smoking," "I will get LASIK," "I will do Invisalign." The declaration fixes the end state and deliberately **leaves the method open** — LASIK or not, Invisalign or not — so identity leads and tactics remain an implementation detail chosen later.

### 2. Narrative ethics — phrase it so it exploits no one
Audit the story each line encodes, down to the preposition. Write "I have the best working relationship with every stakeholder" and "the best talents working for the corporation, and they are extremely happy and motivated" — never "the best talents working **for me**." Even in a private document nobody sees, ownership grammar trains an extractive posture; stewardship grammar trains the leader you become. Same rule for friends: declare who they are ("my friends are trustworthy, generous, and loving people"), not what they give you.

### 3. The reading ritual — daily, for a season
Read the full declaration set every single day for a season. The repetition is what turns written lines into anchors; it is a ritual with a duration, not a permanent obligation.

### 4. The causal theory — hard anchors, not magic
Declarations work because "I move myself towards it" — nothing mystical. Once written and rehearsed, each declaration is a **hard anchor**: a fixed reference point against which you can measure whether your life is actually moving. The source's receipts: nearly everything written in this style since 2010 has happened or is visibly happening, including declarations that seemed crazy when written.

### 5. The hard-anchor audit
Periodically (the source models both a yearly revisit and quarterly checks), go through the set and sort every declaration into:
- **Signs of happening** — measurable movement toward the state; leave it running.
- **Glaring gaps with known fixes** — stalled, but the intervention is already known; these go into the engineering loop below.

### 6. The engineering loop — gaps are a backlog, not a self-judgment
For any stalled declaration (taught on health, applies anywhere), run four steps:
1. **Declare the end state** — the present-tense declaration.
2. **Confess the current state without cosmetics** — the real numbers and the real inactivity, given honestly to yourself and to your AI.
3. **Enumerate the purchasable/schedulable fixes** — most gaps are logistics, not willpower: "everything can be done."
4. **Instrument the tracking** — wire in measurement (smart scale, photo-based calorie estimates, sleep logs) before optimizing.

### 7. The grounding worldview — open-world game, not movie script
The belief underneath the whole practice: fate is not fixed — "a person can change it." Heaven didn't cast you in a movie with a scripted role; it created a game — GTA (open-world freedom), then SimCity (you build systems), really an MMO (other real players, quests, consequences). If life is a sandbox, declaring an end state and moving toward it is legitimate gameplay, not delusion. You are a player with build choices, not an NPC of your own life; setbacks are failed quests, not identity verdicts. Agency is the default assumption. (The source extends this into simulation/reincarnation speculation — hold that part loosely; the practical core is the agency model.)

## When to apply
- Annual goal setting, a new life season, or any time the user is writing down goals or a vision.
- Whenever the user states a goal as a task, target, or resolution ("I will…", "I want to…", "my goal is to hit X") — convert it.
- On the review cadence: quarterly/yearly hard-anchor audits of the existing declaration set.
- When a declaration has been stalled for a season: trigger the engineering loop.
- When the user talks fatalistically ("that's just not who I am," "it's too late for me") — apply the open-world reframe: what would this character need to level to get there?

## How your AI uses this
1. **Coach goals into declaration form:** whenever the user states a goal, rewrite it with them as a present-tense state of being ("I am…", "I have…") that leaves the method open. Never store a raw task or numeric target as a north-star item.
2. **Challenge task-shaped or hedged declarations:** "I will try to…", "I hope to…", "I'm working on…" are not declarations. Push back until the line reads as a state of being. When reviewing a rewrite, ask: *is this truer, or just safer?* — hedging that makes a declaration more comfortable is a downgrade, not a refinement.
3. **Run the narrative-ethics audit:** scan every declaration for extractive framing (people working *for me*, what others provide *to* the user) and propose stewardship phrasing ("working relationship with every stakeholder," "talents working for the corporation").
4. **Maintain the ritual:** prompt the daily read for a season; treat the declaration document as the user's north-star context file, distinct from history (roots) and current season.
5. **Audit the hard anchors:** on the review cadence, report which declarations show signs of happening and which are glaring gaps with known fixes — movement measured against the anchor, never a rewrite of the anchor to match reality.
6. **Run the engineering loop on stalled declarations:** elicit the uncosmeticized current state, build the purchasable/schedulable fix list, and set up tracking before optimizing. Treat gaps as backlog items, not character flaws.
7. **Default to agency:** never treat an ambitious declaration as delusional — under the open-world model, declaring and moving is legitimate gameplay. Frame setbacks as failed quests, and "not who I am" objections as build choices.
