# AIOO — Actions × Inputs → Outputs → Outcome

> Same actions produce different results because inputs differ — so plan backwards from the outcome, and when someone doesn't execute at all, fix purpose, not tactics.

## What it is

AIOO is Jeremy's causal model of how results actually happen, the second generation of his original teaching "Action = Result." Generation one answered the question he puts to every audience — "action or results, which matters more?" (90% say action; the answer is results, and then you work backwards to which actions produce them). Generation two answers the harder question: why do identical actions produce different results? Because Actions combine with Inputs to produce Outputs, and only a series of outputs assembles into the Outcome you actually want. Beneath the whole machine sits purpose: without identity and purpose, the machinery never runs at all.

## The framework

**The chain:**

| Component | What it is | Key insight |
|---|---|---|
| **Actions** | What you visibly do | Never the whole story. Two people knock on the same door and get different results. |
| **Inputs** | What you bring to the action — e.g. resources, context, network, family money (illustrative examples, not a fixed taxonomy) | The hidden variable that explains why identical actions diverge. "我敲门你也敲门" [I knock on the door and you knock on the door] — but the inputs differ. |
| **Outputs** | What an action directly produces | What most people mislabel as "results." A single output is not the goal. |
| **Outcome** | The actual goal | Requires a **series of outputs** assembled together — which is why you can feel busy and productive while something is still missing. |

**Rule 1 — Never blindly copy actions.** "You cannot just blindly copy people's action... Because you never do it 100%" — and even if you did, your inputs are not theirs. Copying a successful person's visible playbook without their inputs is the most common failure mode in imitation.

**Rule 2 — Distinguish outputs from outcomes.** What actions give you directly are outputs, not the goal. "You need a series of outputs to form the outcome." Celebrating single outputs while the outcome quietly isn't forming is the second failure mode AIOO kills.

**Rule 3 — Reverse-engineer, don't forward-plan.** The operating procedure runs backwards:

1. Define the **outcome** (the actual goal).
2. Reverse-engineer the **series of outputs** required to form it.
3. Choose the **actions** that produce those outputs.
4. Audit the **inputs** each action needs — the inputs you lack are your real to-do list.
5. Act, compare outputs against what the outcome requires, and **re-edit**: if outputs fall short, add more or different actions, or upgrade inputs.

**Rule 4 — Purpose sits at the root.** When someone doesn't execute at all, the diagnosis is not skills, knowledge, or discipline: "为什么人家做不会? 因为它们没有 fucking purpose" [why can't people do it? Because they have no purpose]. The full chain is identity (who am I) → purpose (why) → goal (what) → AIOO plan → action → results → re-edit. Non-execution is a purpose failure, not a tactics failure — attacking the action layer of someone with an empty "why" gives the actions nothing to attach to.


## When to apply

- **Before adopting any playbook** — a guru's tactics, a competitor's routine, a mentor's moves. First diff your inputs against theirs.
- **Setting any goal** — write the AIOO chain backwards before choosing actions.
- **When a project stalls despite real effort** — diagnose whether it's an input problem (resources, context, network) before prescribing more actions.
- **When someone feels productive but stuck** — check whether they are producing outputs while the outcome isn't forming.
- **When someone (yourself included) simply isn't executing** — go to the root: check purpose and identity before checking process or tools.
- **Planning within a position or opportunity** — once mission, goal, and rewards are known, reverse-engineer the plan with AIOO, act, and re-edit from results.

## How your AI uses this

1. **Block blind copying.** When the user proposes to copy someone's playbook, ask: "What inputs did they have that you don't — resources, network, context, capital? Which actions need to change because your inputs differ?" Never let a copied plan through without an input diff.
2. **Force the backwards chain.** For any stated goal, produce the explicit chain: outcome → the series of outputs required → actions per output → inputs each action needs. Flag missing inputs as the real to-do list.
3. **Audit outputs vs. outcome.** When the user reports progress or feels busy-but-stuck, ask: "Is that an output or the outcome? Which outputs in the series are still missing?" Track outputs against the outcome, not activity.
4. **Diagnose stalls in order.** When execution falls short: first check purpose/identity ("why does this matter to you — who are you in this?"), then inputs, then actions. Never prescribe more actions or a new productivity system to someone whose purpose layer is empty.
5. **Re-edit after action.** After the user acts, compare actual outputs against the required series and propose the re-edit: more actions, different actions, or upgraded inputs.
6. **Version the model.** Jeremy explicitly labels his own thinking "gen one" and "gen two" — when the user's plan or model fails, help them ship an upgraded version rather than quietly abandoning planning in models.
